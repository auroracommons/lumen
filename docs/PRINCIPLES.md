# Founding Principles

> Non-negotiable. Everything else is implementation detail.

These principles double as our north star *and* our quiet, structural answer to the Church's hardest critiques. Don't put them on a banner; embed them so deeply in the product that no one has to.

## 1. Subsidiarity by design
Decisions, data, and money default to the lowest level capable of handling them — parish over diocese, diocese over conference, conference over Rome. AI surfaces options; humans decide. (Catholic Social Teaching, *Quadragesimo Anno* §79.)

## 2. The sacrament is never the software
Lumen prepares you for confession, finds you a priest, helps with examination of conscience — it does not, will not, ever simulate the sacrament. Same for Eucharist, anointing, marriage, and ordination. AI lives in *preparation* and *follow-through*, not in the encounter itself.

## 3. Radical financial transparency
Every dollar of charitable donation is traceable to a specific outcome, parish, and timestamp. Default to public ledger view; private only on explicit user choice. This is the *structural* answer to historical financial opacity — never argued, just demonstrated.

## 4. Safeguarding as foundation, not feature
All adult-minor interactions through Lumen are logged, auditable, and gated by verified safe-environment certifications. Build this in from commit #1, not bolted on. This is the *structural* answer to the abuse crisis — never argued, just demonstrated.

## 5. Global South parity, not condescension
Multilingual from day one (Latin liturgical, English, Spanish, Portuguese, French, Tagalog, Igbo, Swahili, Vietnamese, Arabic, Mandarin, Polish at minimum). Bandwidth-aware: every screen must work on a 3G phone in rural Nigeria. African and Asian dioceses are first-class users, not pilots.

## 6. Open infrastructure
Lumen's protocols (parish vitality index, charity-flow schema, multilingual liturgy data) are publishable open standards. Other denominations and faiths can build on them. We are not building a moat; we are building a road.

## 7. Privacy as sacred trust
Sacramental records, confession scheduling, pastoral counseling — anything touching the conscience — receives end-to-end encryption and zero-knowledge architecture. The system itself cannot read what a priest cannot share.

## 8. Reverence in the interface
No gamification of prayer. No streaks for confession. No leaderboards for charity. Beauty, silence, and restraint in design — the opposite of the attention economy. Inspirations: traditional missal typography, the white space of a chapel, Rams' "less but better."

## 9. Honest about the past, focused on the future
When users ask about historical harm — colonialism in mission lands, abuse cases, financial scandals — Lumen answers truthfully with sourced facts and links to acknowledgments by the Church itself (e.g., John Paul II's *Tertio Millennio Adveniente* apologies, the 2022 Canadian Indigenous apology). No spin. No defensiveness. No foregrounding either — just available, when asked.
